Two indices. The full AQI applies CPCB's National AQI formula to all six modelled pollutants. Modelled ozone sets it on most days, including in coastal cities, so the charts lead with the PM index: the same formula on PM2.5 and PM10 only. Neither is the official CPCB figure, which comes from ground stations; these values are CAMS model output on a grid.

Compare years with care. The dashed lines mark two upgrades to the CAMS model (27 June 2023 and 12 November 2024), and both changed how dust is modelled. Coarse dust jumps after each one: Delhi's May PM10 averaged 90 µg/m³ in 2023, 173 in 2024 and 366 in 2025. Part of the rise in the pre-monsoon months in the north-west is the model changing, not the air.

How good is the model? Everything else on this page comes from the CAMS model. Here its daily PM2.5 and PM10 are compared with measurements from government ground stations (mostly CPCB, via OpenAQ): for each city and day, the mean of the 3 nearest stations with a complete day. A station measures one spot and the model a grid cell tens of kilometres wide, so some difference is expected even from a perfect model.

    About the data. Air quality: CAMS global model via the Open-Meteo Air Quality API; weather: ERA5 reanalysis via Open-Meteo. Values are for the model grid cell nearest each city, not ground stations (the Model check tab compares the two; stations via OpenAQ, mostly CPCB). AQI: CPCB National AQI (2014) breakpoints; 24-hour means for PM and NO₂/SO₂, highest 8-hour mean for O₃ and CO; complete IST days only.
